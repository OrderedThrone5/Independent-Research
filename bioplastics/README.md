## Question

Can algae replace petroleum in everyday plastics, and which species makes the best one? We compared five green algae and two brown seaweeds on three properties that matter for a real plastic: how fast it breaks down, how strong it is and where it melts.

**Hypothesis:** sugar kelp (*Saccharina latissima*) would biodegrade fastest, have the most plasticity and have the lowest melting point.

## Method

- **Green algae** (*Closterium*, *Chlorella*, *Scenedesmus*, *Volvox*, *Spirogyra*) were grown in separate containers of dechlorinated water at 22 °C under identical fluorescent lighting, with 0.25 mL of fertilizer per week. The biomass was harvested by flocculation and processed into plastic films.
- **Brown algae** (*Saccharina latissima*, *Alaria esculenta*) were dried and mixed with water, glycerol and cornstarch.
- Each group was tested in four trials:
  - **Biodegradation:** 0.5 g samples were immersed in water and weighed on an analytical scale
  - **Tensile strength:** measured with calipers and a Newton spring meter
  - **Melting point:** measured with a hot plate and thermometer

## Results

Averages of four trials per species:

| Species | Type | Melting point (°C) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Biodegradation (% per day) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ***Saccharina latissima*** (sugar kelp) | Brown | **65** | **0.60** | **1.21** |
| *Alaria esculenta* | Brown | 71 | 0.46 | 0.98 |
| *Closterium* | Green | 81 | 0.43 | 1.18 |
| *Chlorella* | Green | 75 | 0.37 | 1.10 |
| *Volvox* | Green | 76 | 0.36 | 1.10 |
| *Scenedesmus* | Green | 77 | 0.40 | 1.02 |
| *Spirogyra* | Green | 71 | 0.40 | 0.99 |
| Composite | | 75 | 0.55 | 1.04 |

## Conclusion

Sugar kelp beat every other species on all three measures, so we rejected the null hypothesis. Brown seaweed contains a lot of alginate, which forms films well and makes the plastic more flexible and clearer. Plastics like this could stand in for conventional plastic in uses ranging from water bottles to PVC-type pipe.
